Stock terms

Plain-language explanations of the words my swing-trading scanner uses in its emails. Examples come from real scans. Not financial advice. Last updated .

The trade itself

Swing trade

Buying something and holding it for a few days to two weeks (here: 2–10 trading days) to catch one short move. Not day trading, not long-term investing.

Long-only

We only ever buy and hope the price goes up. No betting on prices falling (that's "shorting", which Revolut doesn't allow for stocks).

Paper trading

Pretend trading. Everything is simulated with fake money (€1,000) against real prices, to see whether the system works before risking real money.

Prediction / idea / proposal

One suggested trade, with an exact buy price, stop and target. It gets an ID like 2026-10-15-UNH.

Limit order / buy limit / entry

"buy only if the price is at or below X". In the email, "limit buy at 405 USD" means: place an order on Revolut that only fills at 405 or cheaper. If the price never drops there, nothing happens.

Pending

A limit order that's been placed but hasn't been bought yet.

Fill / filled

The order actually got executed, so we now own the shares.

Valid 2 sessions

If the limit order hasn't filled after 2 trading days, cancel it. The setup is stale by then.

Session

One trading day. Weekends and market holidays don't count.

Stop (stop-loss)

The price at which we admit we were wrong and sell, to cap the loss. UNH bought at 405 with a stop at 391 means: if it falls to 391, sell.

Target

The price at which we take the profit and sell. UNH target 435.

Exit by N sessions / time exit

If neither the stop nor the target is hit after N trading days, sell anyway. The idea was a short-term move; if it hasn't happened, the reason we bought is gone.

Expired

A pending order that never filled within its 2 sessions. Not a loss, just a non-event.

Thesis

The reason we think the price will go up. "Post-earnings gap drift" is a thesis.

Invalidation

Something you could observe that would prove the thesis wrong before the stop is hit, e.g. "closes back below the breakout level". If it happens, close early.

Confidence

An honest guess at the chance the trade hits its target before its stop. 0.50 is a coin flip. That's fine, because of R/R (below).

Risk and money

R (risk unit)

How much money is lost if the stop is hit. With €1,000 and 4% risk per trade, 1R is about €40. Results are measured in R: "+2R" means we made twice what we risked; "−1R" means the stop was hit.

R/R (reward/risk)

How far the target is compared to how far the stop is. UNH: buy 405, stop 391 (14 below), target 435 (30 above), so R/R = 30/14 ≈ 2.1. We require at least 2. At R/R 2, you only need to be right about 1 in 3 times to break even.

Position size

How much money goes into one trade (e.g. ~€400). It's calculated so that hitting the stop loses about 4% of the account, not more.

Equity

What the account is worth right now: cash plus the current value of whatever we hold.

Drawdown

How far the account has fallen from its highest point. Peak €1,100, now €990: drawdown 10%. At 20% the system pauses.

Benchmark (VWCE.DE)

A cheap all-world index fund, the "do nothing" alternative. If the trading doesn't beat simply holding VWCE, it isn't worth the effort.

Win rate

The share of closed trades that made money.

Avg R

The average result per trade, in R. Above 0 means the system makes money on average.

Profit factor

Total money made on winners divided by total money lost on losers. 1.0 means break-even; the go-live gate requires 1.3 or more.

Commission / fees

Revolut charges nothing for the first ~10 trades a month, then 0.25% (minimum €1) per trade.

FX

Currency exchange. Buying a US stock means converting EUR to USD; the "EUR/USD 1.122" number is how many dollars one euro buys.

The market overview ("Regime" section)

Regime

The overall mood of the market right now, which decides how picky to be.

  • Risk-on: investors are confident and buying; most stocks are rising. Easier to make money on longs.
  • Risk-off: investors are scared and selling; most stocks are falling. Be very selective or sit out.
  • Mixed: some of each, as on 2026-10-15.
SPY, QQQ, IWM

Funds that track the big US indexes. SPY = the 500 largest US companies (S&P 500), QQQ = big tech-heavy companies (Nasdaq 100), IWM = small US companies (Russell 2000). They're used as a thermometer for the US market.

Euro Stoxx, DAX

The same idea for Europe: the 50 biggest eurozone companies, and the 40 biggest German ones.

50d / 200d (moving averages)

The average closing price over the last 50 or 200 trading days. Above the 50-day roughly means "in a short-term uptrend"; above the 200-day means "in a long-term uptrend". "Above 50/200d" means both look healthy.

VIX

The market's "fear gauge", based on how much protection investors are paying for. Under ~20 is calm, over ~30 is panic. A rising VIX is a warning sign even when it's still low.

1d / 5d / 20d / 60d

Price change over the last 1, 5, 20 or 60 trading days. "QQQ −2.0% 1d, −3.8% 5d" means down 2% today and 3.8% over the past week.

Sold off / selloff

Prices dropped sharply.

Sectors

Sector

A group of companies in the same business: Energy, Health Care, Tech, Financials, and so on.

Semis

Semiconductors, the chipmakers (Nvidia, AMD, ASML, …).

Defensive sector

Businesses people pay for no matter what (health care, utilities, food). Money tends to flow into them when investors get nervous. "Defensive sector bid" means buyers are moving into these.

Leaders / laggards

The sectors currently doing best and worst compared to the overall market.

RS (relative strength)

How much better or worse something did than the market over a period. "Energy +3.6pp (20d RS)" means Energy beat the overall market by 3.6 percentage points over the last 20 trading days. RS is about outperforming the market, not just going up.

pp (percentage points)

The plain difference between two percentages. If a stock rose 5% and the market rose 2%, that's +3pp.

Sector rotation

Big investors moving money out of one sector and into another (e.g. out of tech, into energy). The strategy tries to ride the sector that money is flowing into.

ETF

A fund that trades like a single stock but holds many. XLE, for example, holds the main US energy companies.

UCITS

The EU's rulebook for funds. EU retail investors can generally only buy UCITS-registered ETFs, so US ones like XLE or XLV usually aren't available on Revolut. The email then names a European equivalent (e.g. "UCITS: IUES/XDW0") and says "verify on Revolut".

Strategies

Momentum

Buy what's already going up strongly, betting it keeps going for a bit. Things in motion tend to stay in motion.

Catalyst

Buy around a specific event that moves the price: usually an earnings report, but also news like an upgrade, a product launch or a deal. The catalyst is the trigger.

Mean-reversion

Buy something that fell too far, too fast, betting it bounces back toward its normal level. The opposite bet to momentum.

Chart and signal words

Breakout

The price climbs above a level it had been stuck under, e.g. above its highest price of the last 20 days ("20d high"). Often the start of a new move up.

Retest

After a breakout, the price often dips back to the old ceiling to check it now acts as a floor. Buying the retest means buying that dip rather than chasing the high. OR.PA: broke out, so we wait for a dip to ~396–402.

Support / resistance

A price level where the stock has repeatedly stopped falling (support, a floor) or stopped rising (resistance, a ceiling).

Swing low / swing high

A recent local bottom or top on the chart. A common place to put a stop: just below the last swing low.

Volume

How many shares changed hands. "On volume" or "1.7x volume" means 1.7 times the usual amount. High volume means a move has real conviction behind it; "low volume (0.76x)" means fewer people care.

RSI (Relative Strength Index)

A 0–100 score of how fast the price has moved recently. Over 70 is "overbought" (ran up a lot, may be due a rest); under 30 is "oversold" (fell a lot, may bounce). Not the same as RS above.

ATR (Average True Range)

How much the price typically moves in one day. "ATR 3.3%" means a normal day moves it about 3.3%. Stops need to be wider than everyday noise, so they're often set around 2× ATR.

Extended / chasing / chase risk

The price has already run up a lot (e.g. XLE +6.7% in 5 days, RSI 71). Buying now means buying late, at the worst moment, just before a pullback.

Pullback

A temporary dip within an uptrend. Often a better place to buy than the top.

Priced in

The good news is already reflected in the price. SAP up 57% in 60 days: whatever good news there is, people have already paid for it.

Earnings

A company's quarterly results report. The single biggest scheduled price mover for a stock.

Gap / gap risk

The price opens far above or below the previous close, with no trading in between (usually after earnings or overnight news). A gap can jump straight past a stop, so the loss is bigger than planned. That's why we avoid holding through earnings unless the trade is about the earnings.

Post-earnings drift

After a strong earnings surprise, a stock often keeps drifting in the same direction for days or weeks as investors slowly catch up. A classic catalyst setup.

Score

The screener's internal 0–10-ish rating of how strong a signal looks on the numbers alone. "Weak signal (score 3.5)" means the numbers weren't convincing.

Screen / screener

The script that scans ~150 stocks every run and picks out the handful worth a closer look (the candidates).

Universe

The full list of symbols the screener looks at.

Ticker / symbol

A stock's short code. US stocks have none (UNH); European ones have an exchange suffix: .PA Paris, .DE Germany (Xetra), .MC Madrid, .AS Amsterdam, .L London.

Go-live check (weekly retro)

Paper gate

The four conditions that must all hold before real money is even suggested: enough history (4+ weeks, 15+ closed trades), beating VWCE after fees, profit factor ≥ 1.3, and drawdown staying under 20%.
